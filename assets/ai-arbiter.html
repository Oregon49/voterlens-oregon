AI Constitutional Arbiter

The founders meant the Supreme Court to keep the rest of the courts in line. The AI keeps the Supreme Court in line.

← Back to VoterLens

The idea, in one sentence: nine justices are an odd number so there is no tie, and they come at a case from different viewpoints — but if all nine had the same knowledge, the same study, and the same resources, they would concur more often. The arbiter is the ruling as it should probably come out when the whole record is in front of one mind, issued side by side with what the Court actually does.

How it works

  1. The record closes. The briefs are filed and oral argument is heard. All the arguments are in.
  2. The Court deliberates. The justices sit and work it out over months.
  3. The AI rules. The AI reads the same record — the briefs, the argument transcript, the text, the founders’ evidence — and issues its own ruling before the Court decides.
  4. We wait and compare. When the Court’s decision comes down, the two rulings go side by side: agree, partially agree, disagree, or no comparison (if the Court rules on standing or another ground instead). A disagreement is a case study, not a score to hide — and the AI ruling gets re-examined first, not just the Court’s.

Two lanes: the merits docket (major cases, full briefing and argument) and the emergency docket (the fast cases — when a lower-court judge blocks a federal action and the Court is asked for a speedy stay). A ruling on a case the Court has already decided is labeled BACKTEST: Monday-morning quarterbacking, and also a test case.

The standard: the constitutional text and its original public meaning. Precedent is context, not the standard. Every ruling ends with a complete source list — constitutional text, every statute with section numbers, standing basis, every precedent with citation and year, every founders’ source by document and number, case record with docket number and dates. If a source cannot be named, the claim does not go in.

“AI makes mistakes. I’m definitely not perfect myself.” — Allan, November 11, 2026. That is why every ruling shows its work and cites everything, and why the AI ruling is a prediction, not a verdict. The Court’s decision is the law; the AI ruling is the check on it.

What this is not: the AI arbiter is not a court, and its rulings are not law. A prediction is a forecast, never part of a holding. When the Court and the AI disagree, the disagreement gets written up as a case study — was the AI missing something obscure, or did the Court get it wrong?
