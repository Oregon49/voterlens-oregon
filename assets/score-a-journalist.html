Score a journalist

Paste a link to one of their articles, or type their name. Either one works. The tool pulls a run of that journalist’s recent text articles and scores each one with the same four checks the Journalist Scoreboard uses for whole outlets: loaded language, quotes vs paraphrase, headline vs story, and anonymous sourcing.

What you get is a bias and flavor signal — whether this journalist has a bias or an opinion dressed as news, or just what their flavor is. It is not a verdict on whether they are credible, and it does not check whether their facts are true. It measures the wording.

The link settles who it is — the name, the outlet, and the story are all on it, so there is no guessing which person it is.

If several people share the name, you will be asked which one — each is shown with its outlet.

What this score can and cannot tell you

  • It measures wording and spin, not truth. A cleanly worded story can still be wrong, and a slanted one can still carry real facts. The score tells you how the journalist reports, not whether the facts check out.
  • Only text articles can be scored. Video-only stories with no transcript, paywalled articles, and pages that block automated reading are listed as unreadable, never scored.
  • Opinion is never scored as news. If the link is an opinion piece, the tool says so and stops.
  • Wire copy has no named journalist on it (Reuters, AP, staff reports) and is never scored as one person’s work.
  • Check 4, corrections, is blank for everyone. Scoring it needs the same stories re-fetched after 48 hours to see whether errors were corrected — it is never guessed.
  • The score rests on the articles it could actually read — usually up to 20 recent ones, often fewer. The number scored is shown on the result, so you can see how much it rests on.
  • Known rough edges: the loaded-language list is a fixed watch list (38 words and phrases) and can trip on substrings — “corruption” trips “corrupt”. The anonymous-sourcing list is 14 phrases and misses other phrasings.
How the scoring works

Each article gets four scores from 0 to 100, then the scores are averaged across the articles scored. Check 1 — loaded language: words from the watch list that tell you how to feel about the facts. Check 2 — quotes vs paraphrase: do people speak for themselves, on the record and named, or does the writer paraphrase them? Check 3 — headline vs story: do the headline’s words appear in the story, and do the central facts come early? Check 5 — anonymous sourcing: how often claims are attached to “sources say” and other unnamed phrases.

Classification (fixed before any scores were seen): Straight reporting — mean of the four checks 85 or higher, no check below 60. Mostly straight — mean 70 or higher, no check below 45. Mixed — spin present — mean 55 or higher. Narrative over news — mean below 55. Unrated — fewer than 3 full-text articles scored. The mean itself is not displayed as a combined score; each check stands on its own.

Same checks, same word lists, and same classification as the outlet scoreboard’s full-day run of November 9, 2026 (121 articles: CNN 92.5, MS NOW 91.9, ABC 91.8, AP 91.4, Fox 90.0 — all five Straight reporting).
