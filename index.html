Ballot measure

Measure 22-216

5-Year Local Option Levy for Jail Operations

Official question: “Shall Lebanon levy $1.06 per $1,000 assessed valuation for 5 years beginning 2027 for jail operations? This measure may cause property taxes to increase more than three percent.”

The levy would reopen and operate the 12-bed Lebanon Municipal Jail, closed since 2023, and fund required medical and mental-health services plus six corrections officers and one supervisor.

City estimate: about $1,658,410 per year and $8,292,051 over five years. The estimate for an average Lebanon property is about $296.91 per year; actual cost depends on assessed value.

YES
The tax is imposed for 2027–2031, and the municipal jail reopens and is staffed.
NO
No new tax; the municipal jail remains closed.

The existing library and Justice Center bond rate of $1.29 per $1,000 is in its final year. A no vote lets that line expire; a yes vote replaces it with the smaller $1.06 levy.

Source: City Council Resolution No. 16 for 2026 and council materials; measure number independently corroborated by the Linn County Libertarian Party’s opposition campaign. Final comparison with the mailed ballot is advised.

    No statewide measures this November

    Oregon has no statewide ballot measures on the November 3, 2026 general election ballot. Local measures can still appear, and each voter’s ballot depends on their address and voting districts.

    How to read a local ballot measure

    What is the measure asking?

    Start with the official ballot title and question. Identify the exact action: approve, renew, repeal, authorize, or prohibit. Then read the explanatory statement for what changes in practice.

    What does a “yes” vote do?

    A yes vote approves the action described in the ballot title. Do not assume “yes” always means a new tax or a policy expansion; it may renew an existing measure.

    What does a “no” vote do?

    A no vote rejects the proposed action. For a renewal, that generally means the current levy or authority ends on its existing schedule rather than continuing under the proposed renewal.

    Who pays?

    Look for the tax base and district boundary. A property-tax measure is paid by taxable properties inside the named district, using assessed value. Other measures may use fees, bonds, or another funding source.

    How much does it cost?

    For a rate stated “per $1,000 of assessed value,” divide the property’s assessed value by 1,000, then multiply by the rate. Assessed value may differ from market value.

    How long does it last?

    Find the start date, end date, or number of years in the official measure. “Renewal” does not mean permanent. If the summary is unclear, use the full ballot title and explanatory statement.

    Where is the official wording?

    Use your county voters’ pamphlet. It contains the ballot title, question, summary, explanatory statement, and any submitted arguments. Read the official text before relying on campaign mail.

    Does every Oregon voter see the same local measures?

    No. City, county, school district, fire district, and other local boundaries determine which measures appear on a voter’s ballot.

    Lebanon Measure 22-216

    5-Year Local Option Levy for Jail Operations

    This is the only measure identified for voters inside Lebanon city limits on the November 3, 2026 ballot.

    What is being asked?

    Whether Lebanon should levy $1.06 per $1,000 of assessed value for five years beginning in 2027 to reopen and operate the 12-bed municipal jail.

    What does “yes” mean?

    The tax is imposed for 2027–2031, and the jail reopens with six new corrections officers, one supervisor, and required medical and mental-health services.

    What does “no” mean?

    No new levy is imposed; the municipal jail stays closed.

    Who pays?

    Taxable properties inside Lebanon city limits, based on assessed value.

    What is the estimated cost?

    The city estimates about $296.91 per year for an average Lebanon property, with actual cost based on assessed value.

    How much would it raise?

    The city estimates about $1,658,410 per year and $8,292,051 over five years.

    Sources: City Council Resolution No. 16 for 2026 and council materials; measure number corroborated by the Linn County Libertarian Party’s opposition campaign. Compare with the mailed ballot before voting.

    How the grades and labels work

    This scorecard reflects ONE constitutionalist interpretation of the U.S. Constitution. It is not a court ruling or a legal fact — every grade links to the actual votes so you can judge for yourself.

    The calculation

    Score = votes matching the stated scorecard position ÷ Yes/No votes cast × 100, rounded to a whole number. Exact half-point ties round down.

    Excused or absent votes are not counted for or against a legislator. A vote from before a legislator took office is also not counted.

    Political-tendency labels

    The labels use two separate signals from the 12 displayed sample votes: preference for a larger or smaller government role in spending, regulation, and private contracts; and preference for greater personal liberty or greater government restriction in the four civil-liberty votes. “Libertarian-leaning” means smaller government on the first signal and greater personal liberty on the second. “Conservative,” “liberal / progressive,” “communitarian,” and middle-ground labels describe the other combinations. Because this is a narrow set of votes, the label is a starting point—not a complete political identity.

    Quiz and lawmaker matches

    The quiz asks how you would vote on the same 12 displayed samples. It compares only the support or opposition answers you give with each lawmaker’s recorded Yes/No votes. Skipped questions, absences, and votes from before a lawmaker took office are excluded. A match percentage is agreement on the questions both you and the lawmaker answered.
